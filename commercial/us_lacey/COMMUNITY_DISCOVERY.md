# Community discovery — U.S. customs brokers

## Goal

Learn which part of the Lacey workflow is genuinely painful before pitching Litoral Trace. Do not lead with a product link. Do not ask the community to beta-test in the opening post.

## Suggested Reddit / broker-community post

**Title:** Phase VII brokers: where is the actual Lacey bottleneck now?

**Body:**

I’m researching the operational side of Lacey Act entries after the Phase VII expansion, specifically the work that happens before filing.

For brokers/import teams handling wood, furniture or other plant-product entries, which part is actually consuming the most time today?

- getting genus/species and country-of-harvest information from the importer/supplier;
- re-keying many invoice/SKU lines;
- reconciling supplier spreadsheets with the commercial invoice / packing list;
- correcting incomplete or conflicting information;
- keeping the supporting evidence organized for later review;
- something else entirely?

I’m working on a document-intake prototype, but I’m deliberately trying to understand the workflow before building around assumptions. I’m not looking to promote a filing service here.

If you handle these entries, even a rough description of where the back-and-forth happens would be very useful.

## Why this wording

- It asks a professional workflow question rather than advertising.
- It gives concrete options without assuming one is the answer.
- It is transparent that a prototype exists.
- It does not claim legal expertise or a production Lacey product.
- It does not include a landing-page link in the initial post.

## Follow-up questions for useful replies

### If they say supplier data is the problem
“Does that usually arrive in a dedicated spreadsheet/template, inside the commercial invoice, or across several documents/emails?”

### If they say line-item re-keying
“Roughly how many lines does a painful entry have, and is the source usually structured Excel/CSV or PDFs?”

### If they say species / harvest country
“Who normally owns the follow-up — broker, importer compliance, buyer, or supplier? And how late in the shipment does the missing value typically surface?”

### If they say reconciliation / contradictions
“Which two sources most often disagree: invoice vs supplier sheet, packing list, PO, product master, or something else?”

### If they say evidence retention
“When you need to reconstruct why a species/origin value was declared, where do you go today — broker system, email, shared drive, ERP, supplier folder?”

## Transition to a private 15-minute interview

Only after a substantive reply:

“Thanks — that’s exactly the process detail I’m trying to understand. Would you be comfortable with a 15-minute call next week? I’d like to map one completed entry end-to-end; no client-identifying information is needed. Happy to keep the conversation purely research-focused.”

## Interview questions (15 minutes)

1. Walk me through the last Lacey entry that required real back-and-forth.
2. What files arrived first? What arrived later?
3. Which fields did somebody have to copy manually?
4. Which fields required supplier/importer follow-up?
5. What happens if a value conflicts across two documents?
6. At what point does the broker discover the problem?
7. Where is the supporting evidence retained afterward?
8. Roughly how many minutes/hours did this one entry consume beyond normal customs filing?
9. If the supplier package arrived already structured and exceptions highlighted, which step would disappear — if any?
10. Would you be willing to walk through one completed historical shipment with redacted data to test that hypothesis?

## Scoring the interview

- **0 — No pain:** Lacey data arrives structured/complete; negligible work.
- **1 — Minor:** occasional follow-up, no meaningful time/cost.
- **2 — Repeated:** recurring re-keying or missing data, but manageable.
- **3 — Material:** repeated follow-up/reconciliation across files and people.
- **4 — Acute:** high-volume/manual workload, delays or dedicated staff/process.

A product signal requires repeated score **3–4** across independent organizations, not one enthusiastic respondent.
